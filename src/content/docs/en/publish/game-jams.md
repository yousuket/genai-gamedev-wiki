## Overview

A game jam is an event where you create and submit a game within a set timeframe. A contest is an event with judges and prizes.

For solo game makers, it is a place to get both a deadline and feedback at once. This article covers:

- Why you should submit, and what case studies have achieved
- How to identify events by duration, judging method, and AI rules
- Steps to read and understand AI usage conditions before submitting
- Examples of scope and time allocation by duration, and a submission checklist
- What judges look for and what to do after submission

For a list of upcoming events, see the [Event Calendar](/en/publish/events-calendar/).

## Why submit

| Motivation | What happens | Case study evidence |
|---|---|---|
| There is a deadline | You narrow your scope and push toward completion | The creator of Capybara was making a game for the first time and reached submission in about two weeks ([case study](/en/cases/browser-capybara/)) |
| You get feedback | Players point out unclear explanations and bugs | Plug & Prosper was told about a bug in the results screen and a mismatch in the instructions after launch, and the creator released a fixed version ([case study](/en/cases/browser-plug-prosper/)) |
| You find your objective position | Rankings by category show your work's strengths and weaknesses | Plug & Prosper was 1st overall but 11th in "Fun" |
| It becomes a chance to share your work | Awards and results introduce your game and yourself to people | Capybara placed 1st among 945 entries at [Vibe Jam](https://vibej.am/) 2026 and won $25,000 |
| It becomes a place to experiment | You can test new models and agents with a deadline | AI Browser Game Jam 4 aims to "experiment with AI-driven development" ([itch.io](https://itch.io/jam/ai-jam-4), as of September 2026) |

A pattern in the case studies: the deadline supports making something small and seeing it through to submission. The creator of Plug & Prosper lined up four interpretations of the theme as prototypes on the first day of the jam, compared the gameplay, and then picked one. All four were submitted.

## Types of events

### Duration

| Duration | Event examples (as of September 2026) |
|---|---|
| 3 hours | Trijam. Held every weekend; the goal is to create in 3 hours. Late submissions are accepted, but only work completed within 3 hours is eligible for 1st place ([Trijam #388](https://itch.io/jam/trijam-388)) |
| 48 hours | Micro Jam ([Micro Jam 064](https://itch.io/jam/micro-jam-064)). [Global Game Jam](https://globalgamejam.org/) is the world's largest event; you make a game in 48 hours. 2027 will be the week of January 25–31 ([About](https://globalgamejam.org/about)) |
| 72 hours | Mini Jam ([Mini Jam 219](https://itch.io/jam/mini-jam-219-nocturne)), AI-only Ultimate AI-Powered Game Jam ([#3](https://itch.io/jam/ultimate-ai-powered-game-jam-3)), All Tools Allowed ([#2](https://itch.io/jam/all-tools-allowed-2)) |
| 1 week | Unity 1 Week Game Jam. A theme is announced on Sunday at 8 PM, and submissions are due the following Sunday at 8 PM ([unityroom](https://unityroom.com/unity1weeks)) |
| 2 to 3 weeks | AI Browser Game Jam 4 (August 1–18, 2026, 126 entries) ([itch.io](https://itch.io/jam/ai-jam-4)) |
| 1 month | [Vibe Jam](https://vibej.am/) 2026 (April 1 to May 1, 2026, 13:37 UTC) ([official site](https://vibejam.com/)) |

### Theme and judging

- The theme is usually announced at the start. Some jams, like AI Browser Game Jam 4, say "the theme is a suggestion; you can ignore it." Others, like Mini Jam, have a separate set of constraints that must always be met
- Judging is usually peer-to-peer voting. Unity 1 Week Game Jam has a one-week voting period after submissions, and results are shown as a list of highly rated works rather than rankings ([unityroom](https://unityroom.com/))
- Vibe Jam 2026 uses judges; prizes include gold, silver, bronze, and 12 special awards ([official site](https://vibejam.com/))
- Global Game Jam is not a competition; participation itself is the goal ([About](https://globalgamejam.org/about))

### AI usage rules

Events have opposite stances on AI.

| Category | Description | Examples (as of September 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| AI required | 90% or more of code is written by AI | Vibe Jam 2026 ([official site](https://vibejam.com/)) |
| AI required | Development is heavily supported by AI, and most of the game is AI-generated | AI Browser Game Jam 4 ([itch.io](https://itch.io/jam/ai-jam-4)) |
| All tools allowed, disclosure required | Write the tools you used on the submission page. If you do not write them, you cannot participate. A submission that is only one prompt can be deleted | All Tools Allowed #2 ([itch.io](https://itch.io/jam/all-tools-allowed-2)) |
| No restrictions | Subject to existing rules like copyright, generative AI is not restricted | Global Game Jam ([AI Policy](https://globalgamejam.org/news/global-game-jam-artificial-intelligence-policy)) |
| Usable but excluded from judging | If AI created most of a category (roughly 90%), you can exclude it yourself from judging | [Ludum Dare](https://ldjam.com/) ([FAQ](https://ludumdare.com/resources/questions/can-i-use-ai/)) |
| Some uses are disqualifying | Image and audio generation AI are prohibited and will result in disqualification | GMTK Game Jam 2026 ([itch.io](https://itch.io/jam/gmtk-jam-2026)) |
| Prize ineligible | Works using generative AI are not eligible for prizes | Trijam #388 ([itch.io](https://itch.io/jam/trijam-388)) |
| Fully banned | Generative AI cannot be used for code, images, audio, text, or thumbnails, and disqualifies if found | Godot Wild Jam ([policy](https://godotwildjam.com/news/godot-wild-jams-policy-on-generative-ai/)) |

Even though they are all "game jams," whether you can submit an AI-made game is completely different. Use the process in the next section to always read and understand the rules before applying.

## How to choose

### Choosing by purpose

| Purpose | Event tendency | Duration range |
|---|---|---|
| I want to experience completing something | Regular jams with many participants and short deadlines. Ones that accept incomplete submissions | 48 hours to 1 week |
| I want to experiment with AI and agents | Jams where AI is required or allowed. AI-only jams share the premise that using AI is itself part of the evaluation | 72 hours to 2 weeks |
| I want to build a following and reputation | Contests with high participant and judge visibility. Your game must run in a browser so you can share the URL | 2 weeks to 1 month |
| I want to win prize money | Contests with prizes. Check AI usage conditions and whether your work is eligible for prizes | Varies by event |
| I want feedback | Jams with active peer voting. If there are few votes, read the comments instead of rankings | 1 week or more |

Scale matters too. AI Browser Game Jam 4 had 126 entries with an average of 8.2 ratings per game and a median of 7 ([results page](https://itch.io/jam/ai-jam-4/results), as of September 2026). Plug & Prosper won with 10 ratings. The creator notes that because there are few ratings, the ranking is not a universal measure of quality ([case study](/en/cases/browser-plug-prosper/)). The meaning of a ranking depends on entry count and rating count.

### Steps to read AI usage conditions before submitting

1. On the jam page, search for "AI," "generative," "assets," and "disqualif," and read all matching paragraphs
2. Identify what is covered. Which does it apply to: code, images, audio, text? Does it cover just the game itself, or also the game's page and thumbnail? (GMTK covers the [itch.io](https://itch.io/) page; Godot Wild Jam covers thumbnails)
3. Distinguish the type of restriction. Disqualification, prize ineligibility, exclusion from judging categories, disclosure requirement, or percentage conditions ([Vibe Jam](https://vibej.am/) requires code be 90% or more; [Ludum Dare](https://ldjam.com/) mentions 90%)
4. If percentage is a condition, decide how to measure. Keep records of commit history, prompt logs, or tools used. The creator of Capybara kept the jam rules in a `.claude/rules` file to avoid disqualification ([case study](/en/cases/browser-capybara/))
5. Read what you can make before the jam. Vibe Jam says games that existed before April 1, 2026 cannot be submitted. GMTK does not allow you to make jam-specific assets beforehand; empty projects and itch.io profiles can be prepared in advance
6. Read the disclosure format. itch.io requires you to accurately list generative AI use on the AI Disclosure tab of the project editing page ([quality guidelines](https://itch.io/docs/creators/quality-guidelines)). All Tools Allowed makes tool names on the submission page a requirement for entry
7. Read the rules page again just before the start and before submission. Answers to community questions can appear in the community tab or Discord
8. Ask the organizer for clarification if anything is unclear, and save answers as screenshots

## Preparation

### Deciding your scope

Duration determines the range you can create. Details are in [Deciding scope](/en/getting-started/scope/). Here are guidelines for jams.

| Duration | Target scope | Do not include |
|---|---|---|
| 3 hours | One action and one win/lose condition | Menu, multiple stages, save |
| 48 hours | One core loop that teaches gameplay in one minute, and a minimal title and results screen | Multiple modes, multiple languages |
| 1 week | Core loop, game feel tuning, several stages or a few minutes of content | Breadth of polish (adding new systems) |
| 2 weeks | Five to ten minutes of content and one to two unique elements | Functions unrelated to the core gameplay |
| 1 month | The above plus tool-making and content depth | Things you can add after the game is done |

The creator of Plug & Prosper noticed that the smallest of the four entries in the same jam placed 1st, reinterpreting "small scope" as "a complete range" ([case study](/en/cases/browser-plug-prosper/)). A narrow, closed range is easier to polish until the end.

### Choosing technology

Most jams ask for a form you can open and play immediately. AI Browser Game Jam 4 requires that you can play in a browser without downloading. [Vibe Jam](https://vibej.am/) 2026 requires no login, free play, no loading screen, and the game starts almost immediately. GMTK requires it to run on Windows PC.

How to choose an engine and structure is in [Web game tech stack](/en/agent-dev/web-game-stack/). Vibe Jam's official recommendation is Three.js, and Capybara is made with Three.js.

### Things to do beforehand

- Create a template repository. Use an empty game to test building, local testing, and publishing from start to finish (GMTK allows you to make an empty project beforehand)
- Test the publishing process with an empty game. Steps for each platform are in [Web game hosting](/en/publish/web-hosting/) and [Publishing on itch.io](/en/publish/itch-io/)
- If you submit to [itch.io](https://itch.io/), put `index.html` at the top of the ZIP, use relative paths, and match file name capitalization. Mismatched capitalization can work on your PC but fail after publishing ([HTML5 games](https://itch.io/docs/creators/html5), as of September 2026)
- Write the jam rules, deadline, and what not to make in your agent's instruction file
- Decide where to keep records of tools, models used, and prompts. You can use these for AI disclosure and explaining your AI usage percentage

### Time allocation examples by duration

The table below is built from case studies and pitfalls in publishing. At the end, always reserve time for submission prep and contingency.

| Duration | Decide and one-shot | Build core gameplay | Tune game feel | Submission prep and contingency |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 hours | 20 min | 100 min | 30 min | 30 min |
| 48 hours | 4 hours | 16 hours | 16 hours | 12 hours |
| 1 week | 1 day | 3 days | 2 days | 1 day |
| 2 weeks | 2 days | 5 days | 5 days | 2 days |

A real example of two weeks is Capybara. Week 1 was the player controller, package physics, smartphone UI, weather, map editor, multiplayer, and other gameplay bones. Week 2 was visual consistency, texture replacement, map polish, seven language versions, and submission thumbnails ([case study](/en/cases/browser-capybara/)). Thumbnails are included as week 2 work.

## Submission checklist

- [ ] The URL opens and you can play right away. No login or sign-up
- [ ] The first startup and load are fast. No long loading screens ([Vibe Jam](https://vibej.am/) 2026 asks you to avoid loading screens and heavy downloads)
- [ ] Open it in an incognito window and without cache
- [ ] Test display and control on both phone and PC. If only one is supported, write that on the page
- [ ] Sound plays. Browsers block audio before user interaction. The Web Audio API AudioContext becomes suspended if created before interaction and must call `resume()` after a click ([Chrome autoplay policy](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/autoplay), [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Media/Guides/Autoplay), as of September 2026). Starting sound with a "Start" button on the title screen is safest
- [ ] If submitting to [itch.io](https://itch.io/), choose embed settings. Turn off Click to play so it starts automatically, but be aware some browsers will mute sound
- [ ] Instructions for controls are in the game or on the page at the start
- [ ] You have screenshots, GIFs, and descriptions. For how to make video, see [Short videos](/en/trailer/social-shorts/)
- [ ] Write licenses and credits for assets and libraries ([Licenses](/en/legal/licenses/)). AI Browser Game Jam 4 asks for credits for third-party assets and tools. [Global Game Jam](https://globalgamejam.org/) requires submitted games to be shared under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 ([AI Policy](https://globalgamejam.org/news/global-game-jam-artificial-intelligence-policy))
- [ ] Write your AI disclosure as required. List which tools you used for code, images, audio, and text
- [ ] Fill in the required fields on the submission form. Vibe Jam 2026 disqualifies games that do not have the official widget script
- [ ] Confirm the deadline time and timezone. Vibe Jam 2026 is May 1, 2026, 13:37 UTC. Japan time is 22:37. Unity 1 Week Game Jam is Sundays at 8 PM
- [ ] Check if you can fix things after submission. GMTK locks uploads during the five-day evaluation period after the jam, so you cannot upload a fixed version. All Tools Allowed also says "uploads lock." Vibe Jam 2026 lets you keep working until the deadline
- [ ] Do not link to another build of your game on the page. GMTK disqualifies if you link to another version
- [ ] Do not post spoilers in your game or page. Judges and players should discover the game fresh

## What judges look for

### Judging in the case studies

- **[Vibe Jam](https://vibej.am/) 2026**: One judge, Tim Soret, said "quality is much higher than last year, and some are close to real games" ([levelsio blog](https://levels.io/vibe-jam-2026-winners-quality), June 17, 2026). Prizes are gold, silver, bronze, and special awards like Most Original, Best Art Direction, and Most Played. The official rules do not list judging criteria ([official site](https://vibejam.com/), as of September 2026). Capybara has game feel chosen by hand, maps built by hand, and small details ([case study](/en/cases/browser-capybara/))
- **AI Browser Game Jam 4**: Categories are Overall, Fun, Graphics, Audio, Theme, and AI Usage. Plug & Prosper scored Overall 4.1, Fun 3.6 (11th), Theme 4.9, Audio 4.6, Graphics 4.5, AI Usage 4.4, taking 1st in all but Fun ([results page](https://itch.io/jam/ai-jam-4/results))

### Fun and Overall are different

Plug & Prosper was 1st overall but 11th in Fun. The creator's other three games ranked 22nd, 39th, and 79th. Ranking is supported by theme, look, sound, and completion—not just game feel.

Meanwhile, the creator of Capybara writes that a convenience store scene they found fun was rated as stiff by judges ([case study](/en/cases/browser-capybara/)). What feels fun to you may not feel fun to players.

### The effect of narrow scope

Plug & Prosper was the smallest of the four and placed 1st. Charge Grid, which was most noticed in a Discord poll beforehand, placed 39th. Narrower scope makes it easier to polish the parts judges and players touch ([common lessons in case studies](/en/cases/lessons/)).

### Align with the organizer's criteria

When criteria are published, follow them exactly. All Tools Allowed #2 has four: immersion, player experience, polish and cohesion, and theme. Mini Jam 219 has four: fun, concept, presentation, and use of restriction. Micro Jam 064 says theme use is the subject of voting.

## After submission

- **How to take feedback**: Separate comments into facts (what happened) and preferences (how you want it). Facts are for fixes. Plug & Prosper fixed a bug in the results screen and a mismatch in instructions after getting feedback ([case study](/en/cases/browser-plug-prosper/)). With few ratings, read the comments instead of the ranking
- **Keep updating and shipping**: Plug & Prosper kept updating after the jam and added daily progression, upgrades, and a customer encyclopedia. [itch.io](https://itch.io/) handles plays, distribution, and updates together ([Publishing on itch.io](/en/publish/itch-io/))
- **From browser to app**: Methods to turn a browser game into an app are in [Web games as apps](/en/publish/web-to-app/)
- **Growth toward [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/)**: If the game is not substantial enough, a choice not to release is valid. The creator of Capybara plays for five to ten minutes, lacks progression feel and hours of content, and chose not to sell on Steam. To develop further, see [Choosing platforms](/en/monetization/platforms/) and [Wishlists](/en/monetization/wishlists/)
- **Find the next event**: Use the [Event Calendar](/en/publish/events-calendar/) and [itch.io's jam list](https://itch.io/jams). itch.io treats posting to unrelated jams as spam for promotional purposes and may revoke submission rights ([quality guidelines](https://itch.io/docs/creators/quality-guidelines), as of September 2026). Submit work that fits the theme

## Tips for submitting with coding agents

Key points from case studies. Details are in the linked articles.

| Tip | Case study evidence | Detailed article |
|---|---|---|
| First one-shot gets something moving; tune game feel by playing | Grumbulus got a playable first version from a one-line concept and grew it in two days of playtesting. Capybara's creator spent more time thinking, planning, and playing than writing code | [One-shot prompting](/en/agent-dev/one-shot/), [From one-shot to product](/en/agent-dev/from-one-shot-to-product/) |
| Narrow your scope | Plug & Prosper's creator could give more detailed instructions to the agent by staying within what they could picture | [Deciding scope](/en/getting-started/scope/) |
| Set up verification | Grumbulus generated multiple files in parallel and found and fixed 18 merge bugs before first play | [Verification loop](/en/agent-dev/verification-loop/) |
| Human decides game feel | Capybara's creator changed package physics to prioritize fun over realism. They also had a slider made for tuning | [Game feel](/en/agent-dev/game-feel/) |
| Pick one unique element | Capybara has handmade maps and small details; Plug & Prosper has a physics-driven cable and five handmade levels | [Bringing out your unique voice](/en/agent-dev/differentiation/) |
| Put rules in the instruction file | Capybara's creator added jam rules to the instructions from the start | [The big picture](/en/agent-dev/overview/) |
| See pitfalls before playing | Many bugs only appear when you publish | [Failure patterns](/en/agent-dev/failure-patterns/), [Playtesting](/en/design/playtesting/) |

The day before submission, you can use a prompt like this:

```text
Do a final check before submission.
- Open the public URL in an incognito window equivalent, and report initial load time and any console errors
- Take screenshots at 390px and 1280px width, and list any overflow or unplayable controls
- Check that sound plays after the first click
- List any mismatch between the README and submission page description and current controls and features
- Output a list of dependent libraries used and asset licenses
I will fix only what I approve.
```
