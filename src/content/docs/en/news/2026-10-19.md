## This week's highlights

### Google releases Gemini 4 Argon

On October 13, Google released Gemini 4 Argon. It is available in AI Studio and the Gemini API, and Google AI Ultra subscribers can also use it in Antigravity. The introductory price is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, for a limited time until November 30, 2026. After that, it will be $4 for input and $20 for output. The official blog shows demos of 3D games rendered in the browser without external libraries, but it has no performance comparison for game development. If you want to use it, trying it during the introductory pricing is the practical choice.

- Source: [Google blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-argon/)

### decomp.dev shuts down, and AI ports and mods surge

The admin of decomp.dev, a community for decompiling and modding games, announced its shutdown on October 15. They said AI had made the work too easy, and that human-led projects could no longer be told apart from AI-generated output (404 Media). Kotaku reported that browser ports and decompile/recompile projects have surged since Claude Opus 5.5, and that many of the DMCA takedown notices published on GitHub involve game companies. Meanwhile, Microsoft was reported to have created "Xbox Labs," a small team that experiments with decompiling games. Following last week's removal cases, the rights risk of porting or modding existing games with AI keeps rising.

- Source: [404 Media](https://www.404media.co/video-game-reverse-engineering-community-shuts-down-because-ai-has-made-it-too-easy/), [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/ai-vibe-coding-just-hit-the-video-game-industry-like-an-avalanche-2000746366), [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/news/1010406/xbox-labs-game-decompiling-emulation-exploration)

### Epic describes its new store as entirely vibe coded

According to Destructoid, Epic says it built its new Epic Games Store (in testing) with AI coding agents only. Epic claims that first launch loads about twice as fast as Steam, but third-party verification is unconfirmed. VGC reported that Epic also told developers of an unannounced game to stop writing code by hand (the original Bloomberg article is unconfirmed). The reports do not describe concrete effects on solo developers, such as changes to Unreal pricing or tools.

- Source: [Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/epic-games-store-ai-vibe-coded/), [VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/epic-games-reportedly-orders-staff-to-implement-ai-first-development/)

### Steam wishlists are "inflated," and conversion has roughly halved

Chris Zukowski analyzed data from 160 titles. Comparing 34 games released in 2020–2022 with 126 released in 2023–2025, the median ratio of sales to wishlists at launch fell by about half at one month and at six months after release. Estimating sales with the old "wishlists × fixed ratio" rule from a few years ago can overstate them.

- Source: [Automaton (in Japanese)](https://automaton-media.com/articles/newsjp/20261017-474077/)

### SQUARE ENIX GAME CONTEST 2026 details

Total prize money is 1 billion yen (300 million yen for the grand prize, 100 million yen each for four Masterpiece awards, and 30 million yen each for ten Excellence awards). Entries are accepted from December 15, 2026 to 23:59 on March 15, 2027, and individuals can enter. For AI, the organizers will provide a separate "AI usage guideline" apart from the entry rules, and it will stay in beta until just before entries open. Its contents are not yet public, so whether AI use is allowed is unknown.

- Source: [Automaton (in Japanese)](https://automaton-media.com/articles/interviewsjp/square-enix-game-contest-20261014-473272/)
