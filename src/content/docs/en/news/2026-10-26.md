## This week's highlights

### About 98% of Unity CLI runs come from coding agents

Unity published usage data for Unity CLI. More than 226,000 logged-in users have used it since July, and about 98% of runs between July 20 and October 13, 2026 came from automation such as coding agents. Only 0.2% were run directly by people. There are three official plugins: Claude Code, Codex, and Grok, and you can set up the Claude Code one with `unity setup claude`. Cursor and Cline can also drive the CLI without a plugin. For using agents with Unity, the CLI is becoming the standard entry point.

- Source: [Unity blog](https://unity.com/blog/unity-cli-usage-data-ai-coding-agents)

### GameMaker's new GMRT runtime becomes free on January 1, 2027

According to Game Developer, GameMaker (owned by Opera) will make its new GMRT runtime free for all developers from January 1, 2027. Until now, you needed a Professional license even to publish to PC, mobile, browser, or GX Games. The article says GMRT includes tools for AI development workflows with Claude Code. Publishing to PS5, Xbox Series X|S, and Switch 2 still requires an Enterprise license.

- Source: [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/gamemaker-s-gmrt-runtime-will-soon-be-free-for-all-commercial-devs)

### AI-assisted decompile ports surge, while wariness of "clones" grows

The Verge reported a surge in ports made by analyzing old games with AI tools and rebuilding them for other environments. Examples include a browser version of Halo and an iPhone version of Wind Waker, and the game preservation community is split. In an individual case, someone gave Claude a zip from archive.org and had it decompile an early-2000s Dutch educational game so it plays in the browser. On the other hand, fearing their game could be cloned first, the developer of Moonlighter canceled the public playtest of its new game and switched to a small Discord-only test. An unofficial Daggerfall online project was also criticized for collecting about $1,253 a month on Patreon, so the rights risk of recreating existing works with AI for profit remains high.

- Source: [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1015430/ai-retro-games-decomp-recomp-port), [bou.ke](https://bou.ke/blog/zoetermeer/), [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/moonlighter-developer-delays-public-playtest-of-next-game-over-concerns-of-ai-cloning), [Automaton (in Japanese)](https://automaton-media.com/articles/newsjp/20261019-474335/)

### Steam's Personal Calendar becomes an official feature

Steam's "Personal Calendar" graduated from Steam Labs and is now an official feature. Based on games you have played for a long time, it recommends, in an 8-week calendar, games that users with similar play habits have wishlisted or are playing. The recommendations are retrained every day. Automaton also reported that the October Next Fest had about 7,200 demos, about 1.7 times June's, and that games disclosing AI use on Steam number about 10,700 in 2026 (SteamDB counts). With competition growing, it pays to settle your release date and store page early.

- Source: [Steam announcement](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/593110/view/710037059469839718), [Automaton (in Japanese)](https://automaton-media.com/articles/newsjp/20261020-474386/)

### More AI jams, with opposite rules by jam

Epic's 2026 Epic AI MegaJam (November 12–15, UE / UEFN, any number of AI agents, $65,000 in prizes) and Unity's Official 2026 Unity Game Jam (starts November 5, generative AI allowed if disclosed) were announced. In contrast, Pixel Forge MEGA JAM (starts November 28) bans all generative AI. Check the rules before you join.

- Source: [2026 Epic AI MegaJam](https://itch.io/jam/2026-epic-ai-megajam), [Official 2026 Unity Game Jam](https://itch.io/jam/official-unity-game-jam-2026), [Pixel Forge MEGA JAM](https://itch.io/jam/pixel-forge-mega-jam)
