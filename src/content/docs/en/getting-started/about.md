## Overview

GenAI GameDev Wiki is a wiki for people who want to make games on their own with generative AI.
It collects what you need from idea to post-release: choosing a genre, game design, dev environment, trailers (promotional videos), monetization, and rights and terms of service.
This article explains the purpose of the wiki, who it is written for, and how to read through the categories.

## Purpose of this wiki

Generative AI has widened what a solo developer can do alone.
Rough code drafts, trial images and sound effects, and brainstorming game ideas all go faster than before.

Some parts still need a human to decide, though:

- What kind of game to make (choosing a genre and a scope)
- Judging whether the game is fun to play (playtesting and tuning)
- Where and at what price to sell, and how to get people to know about it
- Whether you are respecting the rights to generated assets and the terms of tools and platforms

This wiki separates "what AI speeds up" from "what AI cannot do for you", and lays out a path for **finishing your first game and taking it all the way to release**.

## Who it is for

| Intended reader | What we skip | What we explain carefully |
|---|---|---|
| An engineer who is new to making games | Git, the CLI, package management, general programming | Game industry terms, game design thinking, asset production, sales and promotion |

Game industry terms such as "core loop (the basic play that repeats in a game)" and "wishlist (Steam's "want to buy" list)" get a one-line explanation the first time they appear in each article.

## Finding your way around the categories

The wiki is split into 11 categories.
If you are new, start with "Getting Started" and read in order; after that, open the categories you need for the stage you are at.

| Category | Contents | When to read |
|---|---|---|
| Getting Started | How to use this wiki, the overall roadmap, how to decide scope | First |
| Game Genres | Which genres suit which people, genres that pair well with AI, genres that use an LLM inside the game | Before planning |
| Game Design | Core loops, MDA, level design, balancing, playtesting, design documents | Planning through production |
| Dev Environment | Game engine comparisons, AI coding tools, asset generation, development workflow | Prototype through production |
| Agent Development | The limits of one-shot prompting, how to stand out beyond it, how to run development, prompt patterns, verification loops, parallel development | Prototype through production |
| Case Studies | Real games built with AI: which model, which prompts, and what came out | Before planning, or when you are stuck |
| Publishing & Events | Where to run and publish: web game hosts, itch.io, lists of game jams and contests | When the finish line is in sight, or when you want a reason to enter a contest |
| Trailers | Trailer structure, capture and editing, AI video generation, Steam trailer requirements, short videos for social media | Before the store page goes live |
| Monetization | Sales platforms, pricing, ads and in-app purchases, wishlists, crowdfunding | Keep it in mind from planning; study it closely before release |
| Rights & Terms | Copyright of AI-generated assets, commercial-use terms of AI tools, platform AI policies, licenses | Before you start using AI tools, and before release |
| Latest News | Weekly reports and the change log | Regularly |

[The overall roadmap from planning to release](/en/getting-started/roadmap/) lays out, stage by stage, what to read in what order.
If you are not sure what to make, first read [How to decide your scope](/en/getting-started/scope/) and [Genres and what suits solo development](/en/genres/overview/).
