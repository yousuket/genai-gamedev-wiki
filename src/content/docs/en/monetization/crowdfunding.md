## Overview

Crowdfunding for games (also called "crowdfunding campaigns") is a way to gather support for a game before it is finished through the internet, and give supporters rewards such as the game itself or special items.
For games, the "rewards-based" format is most common.

This article covers the following:

- Conditions and fees for using Kickstarter and CAMPFIRE from Japan (as of September 2026)
- What to prepare before you begin
- Common failure patterns and the basics of tax treatment

## Crowdfunding is about building an audience, not just raising money

For solo developers, crowdfunding has three main purposes:

- **Development funding**: To cover outsourced work (music, art, and so on) or tool costs.
- **Demand verification**: To confirm how many people want the game enough to pay for it.
- **Community building**: Supporters become your first fans who will cheer you on even after release.

However, **crowdfunding works best if you already have fans**. On Kickstarter, if you do not reach your goal, you do not get the funds, so "just telling people about it" does not guarantee backers will appear. Building an audience on wishlists and social media comes first (see [How to grow wishlists](/en/monetization/wishlists/)).

## Comparison of major platforms

| Item | Kickstarter | CAMPFIRE |
|---|---|---|
| Main backers | Worldwide (English-speaking regions are dominant) | Japan only |
| Available from Japan | Yes (with conditions) | Yes |
| Method | All-or-Nothing: funds received only if goal is reached | All-or-Nothing or All-in (receive funds even if goal is missed) can be chosen |
| Fees | 5% + payment processing fee of 4.5% (Japan projects) | 12% + payment processing fee of 5% (before tax) |
| If goal is not reached | Backers are not charged, and no fees apply | All-or-Nothing: no fees apply |

(as of September 2026; sources are in the sections below)

Japan also has other services such as Makuake and READYFOR.

## Kickstarter

- **Conditions for use from Japan**: Japan is listed as a country where you can create projects. As an individual, you must be 18 years old, living in Japan, hold a government-issued ID from Japan, have a Japanese bank account in your name, and have a credit or debit card in your name ([Who can use Kickstarter](https://help.kickstarter.com/hc/en-us/articles/115005128594-Who-can-use-Kickstarter), as of September 2026).
- **Fees**: If your project succeeds, Kickstarter takes 5% and payment processing fees are deducted. For Japan projects, the payment processing fee is 4.5% ([Fees: Japan](https://www.kickstarter.com/help/fees?country=JP), as of September 2026).
- **All-or-Nothing**: If you do not reach your goal, backers are not charged. Kickstarter calls this "how we differ from a store" ([Why is funding all-or-nothing?](https://help.kickstarter.com/hc/en-us/articles/115005047893-Why-is-funding-all-or-nothing)).
- **AI disclosure**: Projects using generative AI must state in the application and on the project page what AI technology is used, how it is used, what is your original work, and what parts use AI output ([Can I use AI-generated content...](https://help.kickstarter.com/hc/en-us/articles/16848396410267-Can-I-use-AI-generated-content-or-create-AI-technology-in-my-project)). Insufficient disclosure may result in your project being stopped ([Kickstarter announcement](https://updates.kickstarter.com/introducing-our-new-ai-policy/)). For AI disclosure on platforms in general, see [Platform AI policies](/en/legal/platform-policies/).
- **Language**: Most backers are outside Japan, so the page and video must be in English.

## CAMPFIRE

- **Fees**: On standard CAMPFIRE, if the project is successful, you pay 12% (service fee) + 5% (payment processing fee) of the total support amount, plus sales tax ([CAMPFIRE fees](https://help.camp-fire.jp/hc/ja/articles/115013873328-CAMPFIRE%E6%8E%B2%E8%BC%89%E6%99%82%E3%81%AE%E6%89%8B%E6%95%B0%E6%96%99), as of September 2026, in Japanese). There are no listing or setup fees ([CAMPFIRE Academy](https://camp-fire.jp/academy/articles/crowdfunding-fees-explained), in Japanese).
- **Method**: With All-or-Nothing, if you do not reach your goal, all support funds are refunded and no fees apply. With All-in, you receive the funds even if the goal is missed (same source as above). If you choose All-in, plan on delivering rewards even if the goal is missed.
- **Entertainment-focused option**: On "CAMPFIRE for Entertainment", the fees (service 0 to 12%, payment 0 to 5%) can be split between the creator and backers ([CAMPFIRE fees](https://help.camp-fire.jp/hc/ja/articles/115013873328-CAMPFIRE%E6%8E%B2%E8%BC%89%E6%99%82%E3%81%AE%E6%89%8B%E6%95%B0%E6%96%99), in Japanese).

## How to prepare

1. **Build something playable first**: A demo or a vertical slice (a small piece of the finished game at release quality) showing the fun at the core helps you gain trust.
2. **Work out your target amount backward**: Add fees, reward production costs, shipping, and taxes to the "absolute minimum you really need".
3. **Keep rewards simple**: At first, focus on items that do not require shipping, such as a digital copy of the game, credit listing, or a soundtrack.
4. **Make a page and a video**: Make it clear what you are building, when it will arrive, and who is making it. See the article on [Steam trailer requirements](/en/trailer/steam-trailer/) for how to make the video.
5. **Tell fans before you launch**: The momentum right after launch matters. Give advance notice on social media or by email.
6. **Set up a [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/) store page in parallel**: This lets people outside your backer list add it to their wishlist.

## Common failure patterns

| Pattern | What happens | How to prevent it |
|---|---|---|
| No fans when you start | Support does not grow and goal is missed | Build interest on social media and wishlists first |
| Goal is too high | Not reached within the time limit | Set the target to what you really need, then add stretch goals to build on it |
| Physical rewards dominate | Production and shipping costs exceed estimates, going into the red | Focus on digital rewards. Get shipping quotes by region |
| Development timeline is too optimistic | Delays erode backer trust | Keep scope small and show a schedule with room to spare |
| No progress updates | Worry and frustration spread | Report progress on a regular schedule |
| AI use is not explained well | Receive criticism or get stopped in review | Explain from the start where you used AI |

[Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/) says Early Access is not "a way to fund development" ([Early Access](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/earlyaccess)). The same is true of crowdfunding: **plan to finish even if support does not arrive**, so you are not left stranded.

## Tax basics

- For individuals, rewards-based crowdfunding support is treated as "business income" or "miscellaneous income". At the time you receive the funds, they are treated as "money received in advance", and counted as revenue when you deliver the rewards (a common approach; see [Yayoi: Do I need to file taxes on crowdfunding income?](https://www.yayoi-kk.co.jp/shinkoku/oyakudachi/crowdfunding/), in Japanese).
- Platform fees, reward production costs, and shipping are tax-deductible expenses (same source as above).
- Delivering rewards may be subject to sales tax.
- Projects that span calendar years become harder to time for accounting.

## How to use AI

- **Translating the page**: AI translation can draft an English version of your Kickstarter page.
- **Creating a budget table**: Have the AI make a budget table including fees, reward production costs, shipping, and taxes, so you can check if your target amount is reasonable.
- **Drafting progress updates**: Using AI to write routine progress messages makes it easier to report on schedule.
- **Drafting AI disclosure text**: Have AI draft a statement laying out "where I used AI and where I made it myself".
