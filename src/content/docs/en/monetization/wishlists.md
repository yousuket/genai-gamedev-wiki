## Overview

A **wishlist** is a feature on [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/) where players can save games they are interested in. When you add a game to your wishlist, you receive notifications at release and during sales. How many wishlists you gather before launch can significantly impact your sales immediately after release.

This article covers:

- Why wishlists matter and when notifications are sent
- How to launch your store page early, participate in Steam Next Fest, release a demo, reach players on social media, and contact streamers
- Benchmarks for "how many wishlists should I aim for" based on public data

## How Wishlists Work

[Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/) sends notifications to wishlist subscribers at the following times (see [Wishlists](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/wishlist), as of September 2026):

| Timing | Details |
|---|---|
| Release | Notifications are sent when Early Access begins and at full release separately |
| Discounts | 20% or more off, must include the cheapest package, duration longer than 8 hours |
| Demo release | One notification per demo, sent any time within 14 days after the demo first goes live (you choose when to send it) |

- Users who receive a notification about the same game within two weeks do not get additional notifications (see [Wishlists](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/wishlist)). It is recommended to space demo notifications and release/discount notifications at least two weeks apart (see [Demos](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/application/demos)).
- "Personalised Coming Soon," launched as a pilot in October 2025 and integrated into the store homepage in June 2026, shows games from your wishlist and recommended games based on your play history, organized by release date (see [Valve announcement (October 2025)](https://steamcommunity.com/games/593110/announcements/detail/507342099205587495) and [store redesign announcement (June 2026)](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/593110/announcements/detail/704394876188361009)).
- In the same June 2026 redesign, the "Popular Upcoming" tab was updated to focus on the most anticipated games for the coming month (see above).

## What to Do

### 1. Publish Your Store Page Early

[Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/) recommends publishing your Coming Soon page "as soon as you are ready, and as early as possible" (see [Wishlists](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/wishlist)). For your first title, a minimum of two weeks before launch is required (see [Onboarding](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/onboarding)).

- Your store page needs a capsule image (the main header image shown on the store), screenshots, a trailer, a description, and tags.
- For how to make a trailer, see [Steam Trailer Requirements](/en/trailer/steam-trailer/).
- Tags are used to organize games in Next Fest and other events, so choose them accurately (see [Steam Next Fest](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest)).

### 2. Release a Demo

Demos are one of the most powerful marketing tools on Steam. In Zukowski's research, the median percentage of demo players who added the game to their wishlist was 19.3%. Most players do not wishlist after playing—and that is okay (see [Most people will play your demo and not wishlist it](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/06/30/nobody-plays-demos-and-that-is-ok/)).

For how to make a demo, see also the demo section in [Pricing](/en/monetization/pricing/).

### 3. Participate in Steam Next Fest

Steam Next Fest is a weeklong event featuring demos from upcoming games. It is held every February, June, and October (see [Steam Next Fest](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest)).

**Eligibility** (see above, as of September 2026):

- You can participate **only once per title**
- Your game's main store page must be live
- You must have a public demo ready by the event start
- You cannot release the full game before the event ends

**Upcoming Schedule** (all times in US Pacific):

| Event | Dates | Submission deadline |
|---|---|---|
| October 2026 | October 19–26 | August 31 (closed) |
| February 2027 | February 22 – March 1 | January 10, 2027 |
| June 2027 | June 14–21 | April 25, 2027 |

(See [October 2026](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest/2026october), [February 2027](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest/feb_2027), and [June 2027](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest/june_2027) documentation.)

- You must submit your demo build for review at least three weeks before the event (five weeks if you want an early press preview) (see [Steam Next Fest](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest)).
- In Zukowski's research, the median wishlists gained during February 2026 Next Fest was 806; the top 5% gained 13,461 or more. Most top games had made their demo publicly available months before the event (see [Making sense of the February 2026 Steam Next Fest](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/04/13/making-sense-of-the-february-2026-steam-next-fest/)). This is an estimate from a survey, not official Valve data.

Since you can only participate once, **choose the event where your demo is most polished and your store page is complete**.

### 4. Share on Social Media

- Short gameplay videos from development (a few seconds to tens of seconds) show what your game does at a glance and work well on social media. For how to make them, see [Short Videos for Social Media](/en/trailer/social-shorts/).
- Always include a link to your Steam store page in your posts, and ask explicitly: "Add it to your wishlist."
- Use [Steamworks](https://partner.steamgames.com/) "Traffic" section to see which traffic sources led to wishlists (see [Wishlists](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/wishlist)). Keep doing what works.

### 5. Contact Streamers and Curators

- **Curator Connect**: A Steam feature that lets you send your game directly to Steam curators (accounts that recommend games) without exchanging emails or keys. Curators see your first three screenshots and a message (see [Curators and Curator Connect](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/curators)).
- **Reach out to streamers**: Find streamers who play games similar to yours and contact them with a short introduction, trailer, and key. Focus on genre fit rather than follower count alone.
- A demo makes it easy for streamers to try your game.

## Wishlist Count Benchmarks (Public Data)

All figures below are from developer surveys compiled by Zukowski (How To Market A Game), not official data.

**Two weeks after store page launch** (survey of 278 games; see [How many wishlists can you expect when you launch your game's "coming soon" Steam page](https://howtomarketagame.com/2021/12/12/how-many-wishlists-can-you-expect-when-you-launch-your-games-coming-soon-steam-page/)):

| Metric | Count |
|---|---|
| Bottom 25% | 38 |
| Median | 111 |
| Top 25% | 407 |

Zukowski notes a strong correlation between early counts and final sales, but also says "fewer wishlists does not guarantee failure" and recommends using these numbers "to decide whether to adjust your strategy" (see above).

**At launch**: Zukowski previously suggested about 7,000 wishlists as a target for appearing on the "Popular Upcoming" tab. After the June 2026 store redesign, his article was updated to focus on Personalised Coming Soon, and the benchmarks changed. Note that these are still preliminary estimates (see [How many wishlists should I have when I launch my game?](https://howtomarketagame.com/2022/09/26/how-many-wishlists-should-i-have-when-i-launch-my-game/)).

:::tip
Instead of worrying about hitting a specific number, track which tactics increased your wishlists and spend time on what works.
:::

## How to Use AI

- **Store page copy**: Have AI generate several versions of your description and tagline, then compare them. This is efficient.
- **Social media drafts**: AI can help you write posts in Japanese and English, making it easier to post regularly. Include concrete details about your development to avoid repetitive text.
- **Streamer lists**: Use AI to organize candidate streamer information into a table. But write your outreach message yourself, tailored to each person. Mass outreach does not work.
- **AI in store assets**: If you use generative AI in your capsule image or trailer, you must disclose it to [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/). See [Platform AI Policies](/en/legal/platform-policies/) for details.
- **Feedback analysis**: Use AI to categorize demo reviews and survey comments to identify improvements. For how to run playtests, see [Playtesting](/en/design/playtesting/).
