## Overview

Short-form videos are an entry point for people who do not yet know your game. Their purpose and how they are watched differ from a store page trailer.

This article covers:

- The differences in video specifications across the main social networks (as of September 2026)
- How to make vertical videos and how to think about the opening one second
- How to share development logs (devlogs) and how to use hashtags
- How much time to spend on social media

## Difference from trailers

| Item | Steam trailer | Short-form video |
|---|---|---|
| Viewers | People who came to the store page with some interest already | People scrolling their timeline, who do not know your game |
| Speed of decision | About 10 seconds ([Steamworks](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/trailer)) | About 1 second before swiping away |
| Screen orientation | Landscape (16:9) | Vertical (9:16) is the norm |
| Goal | Wishlist registration or purchase | Follow or navigate to store page |

## Video specifications by platform

| Platform | Length limit | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| YouTube Shorts | 3 minutes | A square or vertical video up to 3 minutes is treated as a Short ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877)) |
| TikTok | In-app recording up to 10 minutes, upload up to 60 minutes | If you pick the audio first, the video length is determined by the audio length ([TikTok Support](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/creating-videos/camera-tools)) |
| Instagram Reels | 20 minutes | Reels over 3 minutes are not recommended to people who do not follow you ([Instagram Help](https://help.instagram.com/2720958398006062)) |
| X | 140 seconds (non-Premium) | Premium subscribers can post longer videos ([X Help](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/premium-longer-videos)) |

The limits are just limits. If your goal is to reach new people, the basic rule across all platforms is to keep it short.

## How to make vertical videos

### Recording

If you crop a landscape recording in the center, information at the edges of the screen is lost. If possible, **record directly at a vertical resolution (1080 x 1920)**. See [Screen capture and editing tools](/en/trailer/capture-and-editing/) for how to add resolution presets to your capture build.

Some games suit vertical screens and some do not.

- Suited: Puzzles, card games, mobile games, games where characters stay in the center of the screen
- Requires work: Horizontal scrollers, strategy games, and other games with a wide landscape screen. Solutions include moving the camera closer, splitting the screen vertically to show "gameplay" and "text explanation" side by side, and other adjustments

### Screen layout

In short-form video apps, captions, buttons, and account names overlap at the bottom or right edge of the screen. **Place important video and text near the center of the screen**. Before you post, check the draft view in the actual app to be sure.

### Opening one second and structure

Chris Zukowski of How To Market A Game introduced tips from developers who succeeded on TikTok ([Seven great tips for marketing your indie game on Tiktok](https://howtomarketagame.com/2022/02/07/seven-great-tips-for-marketing-your-indie-game-on-tiktok/), 2022):

- Hook the viewer within about **one second** before they swipe
- Make the content understandable without prior knowledge (viewers do not know you or your game)
- Keep it short and make it a **loop** that flows naturally from end to start
- Include a call to action such as "follow" or "see the link in my profile" visible for at least three seconds
- Do not overwork it; keep the handmade feeling

Examples of an opening include "start with the most satisfying moment", "pose a question like 'Can you beat this?'", or "show an unlikely situation (a swarm of enemies, gigantism, and so on) first".

## Sharing development logs (devlogs)

A devlog is a post that shows your development process. You can share it even before the game is finished, and it conveys your character as a creator.

| Idea | Content |
|---|---|
| Before / after | Changes from placeholder assets to finished version, before and after adding effects |
| New feature | Show a newly implemented system briefly |
| Fun bug | Physics engine runaway and other things that are fun to watch |
| Behind the scenes | Editor screens, how you work with AI tools |
| Question | A poll like "Which design do you prefer, A or B?" |

Development using AI tools tends to become a topic on its own, but people respond to it differently. The safest approach is to describe honestly how you use it.

:::caution
When you post realistic AI-generated video or audio, YouTube and TikTok may require disclosure or labeling. For details, see [Using AI to generate video](/en/trailer/ai-video/).
:::

## Hashtags

There are hashtags that have taken root among game developers.

| Hashtag | How to use |
|---|---|
| `#screenshotsaturday` | A habit of sharing screenshots, GIFs, or videos from development every Saturday. On X, accounts like [@ScreenshotSatRT](https://x.com/ScreenshotSatRT) share posts. Bluesky also has a [Screenshot Saturday feed](https://bsky.app/profile/trezy.codes/feed/screenshot-sat) |
| `#indiedev` / `#gamedev` | Solo development and game development in general |
| `#indiegame` | Reaches the player side more easily |
| `#madewithunity` / `#godotengine` and others | Engine communities |

Narrowing to a few related hashtags makes your content more understandable. Tags for developers reach other developers but do not always reach the player base. When you want to reach players, include words players use, such as genre names.

## How much time to spend on social media

Sharing on social media is not required. Chris Zukowski points out that games sell even with zero followers, and recommends spending time on **submitting to festivals** and **reaching out directly to streamers and YouTubers** instead of posting on social media. He also notes that most hit games gained followers after the hit, not before launch ([Can you market a game with zero following?](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/07/15/can-you-market-a-game-with-zero-following/), 2025).

A realistic approach is:

1. Build a habit of making short videos from recorded material between development work (do not spend a lot of time per video)
2. Note which videos got good reactions and apply the same patterns to your trailer opening
3. Stop formats that do not keep getting reactions
4. Check whether your final goal (wishlists) is being reached

For the full picture of how to gather wishlists, see [How to gather wishlists](/en/monetization/wishlists/).

## How to use AI

- **Finding candidates for clips**: Searching through long recordings for highlights is tedious. Keep a play log (when events happen), and have an LLM extract "time spans where dramatic events cluster". This speeds up finding material.
- **Caption and hashtag ideas**: Use it to draft post text or translate to other languages.
- **Weekly review**: Collect views and follow counts from each post and have the AI analyze the trends.
- **Add one line yourself**: Fully automated text tends to repeat the same wording. Just adding one line in your own words changes the impact.
