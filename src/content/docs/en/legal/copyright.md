## Overview

When using images, music, or text created by generative AI in a game, consider these two as **separate issues**.

1. **Infringement risk**: whether the AI-generated output infringes on someone else's copyright
2. **Copyrightability**: whether you own the copyright to the AI-generated output (whether you can protect it if someone copies it without permission)

This article clarifies the Japanese approach (Agency for Cultural Affairs materials and Article 30-4 of the Copyright Law) and the U.S. Copyright Office's position. It then summarizes the risks when output resembles existing works and what measures solo developers can take.

## Japanese legal framework

In March 2024, the Agency for Cultural Affairs published "[Thinking on AI and Copyright](https://www.bunka.go.jp/seisaku/bunkashingikai/chosakuken/pdf/94037901_01.pdf) (in Japanese)" from the Subcommittee on Legal Systems, Copyright Council, Cultural Deliberation Council. In July of the same year, it also released a practical "[AI and Copyright Checklist & Guidance](https://www.bunka.go.jp/seisaku/chosakuken/pdf/94097701_01.pdf) (in Japanese)" for practitioners. Both are consolidated on the [Agency for Cultural Affairs' AI and Copyright page](https://www.bunka.go.jp/seisaku/chosakuken/aiandcopyright.html) (in Japanese) (as of September 2026).

The "Thinking" is not legally binding. It simply presents the council's interpretation of how current law should be understood.

The "Thinking" separates the following two stages.

| Stage | Main Activities | Related Provisions |
|---|---|---|
| AI development and training | Collecting works as training data, creating models | Article 30-4 of Copyright Law, etc. |
| Generation and use | Generating images with AI, publishing or selling them | Standard copyright infringement assessment |

Game developers are typically parties at the "generation and use" stage. However, if you collect works by a specific artist to create a LoRA (a lightweight method of additional model training), you become a party at the training stage as well.

### Article 30-4 of the Copyright Law (training stage)

[Article 30-4 of the Copyright Law](https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/345AC0000000048) (in Japanese) permits uses without authorization for purposes other than "enjoying" the thoughts and emotions expressed in works (non-enjoyment purposes). Collecting data for AI training generally falls under this.

However, the "[Thinking" summary](https://www.bunka.go.jp/seisaku/bunkashingikai/chosakuken/pdf/94057901_01.pdf) (in Japanese) states that Article 30-4 does not apply in the following cases.

- **Intentional "overfitting" and similar cases**: additional training intended to output content with creative expressions common to works in the training data
- **Targeting specific creators**: collecting works with the intent to output creative expressions common to a small set of works
- **Some RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)**: when used with the intent to output the exact expression of existing works
- **Cases covered by provisos**: when the copyright owner's interests are unfairly harmed. For example, unauthorized reproduction of databases provided for a fee for AI training

"Art style" itself is not protected if it is merely an idea. However, if a creative expression common to works is directly perceived in the generated content, it can constitute infringement.

### Infringement assessment at the generation and use stage (similarity and dependence)

When publishing or selling something generated by AI, it is assessed for infringement using the **same standard** as if a person had created it. Infringement occurs when both of the following conditions are met.

- **Similarity**: the creative expression is common with an existing work
- **Dependence**: the output was created based on an existing work

The AI-specific issue is dependence. The "Thinking" clarifies this as follows.

- If an existing work was included in the AI's training data, dependence is **usually presumed even if the user did not know about that work**
- Even if it is unclear whether the work was in the training data, if the copyright holder can show that the user had access to that work or that there is high similarity, dependence can be presumed
- If you include a work name or character name in a prompt, or input an existing image (image-to-image), dependence is more likely to be recognized

Additionally, while generating and enjoying something privately is lawful, **publishing or selling it requires separate consideration**. Incorporating into a game constitutes distribution, so "being able to generate it" does not mean you can use it.

### Copyrightability of AI-generated content

The "Thinking" clarifies the following.

- Output created with minimal human instruction, just pressing the "generate" button, does not qualify as a copyrightable work
- When a person uses AI as a "tool," the output becomes copyrightable, and the user becomes the author
- This determination looks at the human's "creative intent" and "creative contribution"

Examples of factors for assessing creative contribution are listed.

| Factor | Evaluation |
|---|---|
| Quantity and content of instructions or inputs | Detailed instructions showing specific creative expression are more likely to be evaluated as contributing. Long instructions that only contain ideas do not have an effect |
| Number of generation attempts | Merely increasing the number of attempts does not have an effect. If you generate repeatedly while viewing the output and adjusting instructions, it may be recognized |
| Selection from multiple outputs | Merely selecting does not have an effect |
| Adding or editing after generation | Parts that humans creatively add or edit are normally copyrightable |

In a real case, the Chiba Police sent for prosecution a person who used an AI-generated image posted on social media without authorization as a book cover, suspecting copyright infringement. This image was created through repeated detailed instructions, and police determined it to be copyrightable (reported by [Jiji Press](https://www.jiji.com/jc/article?k=2025112000852&g=soc)). Note that this is a police determination, not a court ruling (as of September 2026).

## U.S. Copyright Office position

If you are selling globally on Steam and similar platforms, understanding the U.S. approach is also important.

- In January 2025, the U.S. Copyright Office published a report "[Copyright and Artificial Intelligence, Part 2: Copyrightability](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf)." It concluded that AI output is protected only when **humans made sufficient expressive choices** ([NewsNet 1060](https://www.copyright.gov/newsnet/2025/1060.html)).
- Prompts alone are normally not recognized as sufficient human control. However, parts where human-created materials remain in the output, and parts involving human selection, arrangement, or editing can be protected.
- The Copyright Office stated that existing legal principles are sufficient, and no new law specific to AI-generated works is needed.
- In the Thaler v. Perlmutter case over registration of autonomously generated works, the U.S. Supreme Court declined to grant certiorari on March 2, 2026. The appeals court decision that "the author must be human" became final ([SCOTUSblog](https://www.scotusblog.com/cases/thaler-v-perlmutter/)).
- The Allen v. Perlmutter case challenging the rejection of registration for an image created with Midjourney had not been confirmed at the trial court as of September 2026 (lawsuit documents are posted on the [Copyright Office's AI page](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/)).

When registering copyright in the U.S., you must disclose AI-generated portions. Procedure details are summarized on the [Copyright Office's AI page](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/).

## Risks and measures for output resembling existing works

### Main risks

- **Unintentional similarity**: output can resemble works included in training data, and infringement can occur even if the user did not know about it
- **Using famous character or work names in prompts**: dependence is more likely to be recognized
- **Removal from stores**: Steam requires that distributed content does not contain illegal or infringing content (see [Platform AI Policies](/en/legal/platform-policies/))
- **Difficulty protecting your own assets**: weak copyrightability makes it harder to assert rights if someone copies your work

Lawsuits between AI companies and rights holders are ongoing. For example, the case where Disney and Universal sued Midjourney was still in proceedings as of September 2026 (see [CourtListener](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/70513159/disney-enterprises-inc-v-midjourney-inc/)).

### Checklist of measures

The [Checklist & Guidance](https://www.bunka.go.jp/seisaku/chosakuken/pdf/94097701_01.pdf) (in Japanese) from the Agency for Cultural Affairs, when rearranged for solo developers' use, includes.

- [ ] Check the terms of service of the AI service you use and follow the conditions for commercial use ([Commercial use terms for AI tools](/en/legal/tool-terms/))
- [ ] Do not include names of existing works, characters, or artists in prompts
- [ ] Do not use others' works as image-to-image input (unless you have permission)
- [ ] Before adopting, use reverse image search to check that it does not resemble existing works
- [ ] Record the prompt, generation date and time, model used, and editing history (to explain that you did not depend on others' work and demonstrate your creative contribution)
- [ ] For important assets (key visuals, main characters, etc.), significantly revise and edit them by hand
- [ ] Do not perform additional training on only one artist's works (such as LoRA)

## How to use AI

- **Automate generation logs**: saving image generation logs (prompt, seed value, model name) with an AI coding tool script can be useful later if you need to explain your process. See also: [AI Coding Tools](/en/dev-env/ai-coding-tools/)
- **Use AI for drafts; finish by hand**: this makes it easier to establish copyrightability and reduces the risk of resembling existing works. See also: [Asset Generation](/en/dev-env/asset-generation/)
